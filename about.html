About

My name is Arshit Sharma. I live in Himachal Pradesh, in the Kangra valley, and I come down to Chandigarh and Mohali roughly twice a month, three days at a time. Mostly to see my friends I have had for years. Mostly we eat.

I like almost everything. My friends once sent back a pasta at Pizza Hut that I had no problem with whatsoever, and that happens more often than it should. I did not really start paying attention to food until I began writing reviews on Google Maps and had to explain myself.

I started this because one meal was good enough that I wanted to say so somewhere that would last. Appreciating a place properly is harder than complaining about one, and it is still the reason this exists.

I work in operations, which mostly means reports and building small tools that make other people's jobs less tedious. Some of that will end up here too.

Why I can say a place was bad

Almost every food account in Tricity sits inside the local restaurant economy. They know the owners, they want the invitations, they need those relationships to keep working. Which is precisely why so few of them will tell you a restaurant was not worth the money.

I am not in that economy. I live around two hundred kilometres away and I am driving home on Sunday. Nobody here knows my family.

That is not a virtue. It is a fact about my situation, and it is the reason the scores on this site can say what they say.

What I am not

I am not a chef and I have never worked in a kitchen. I have no formal training in food, and reading about a technique is not the same as knowing it. The best thing I have ever eaten is a plate of peri peri fries at a place in Chintpurni, two hundred rupees, and I hold that maggi between two slices of grilled bread is a genuinely good sandwich.

What I can tell you is whether the thing in front of me was worth eating, worth paying for, and whether I would order it a second time. That is the entire promise here, and I try not to claim anything past it.

The rules

I do not take money to write about a restaurant, and I do not take money to remove one.

I score against fixed criteria, not by feel. Four numbers, thirty points, and the working is published on every review so you can disagree with it properly. How I rate sets it all out.

The Coke Zero

I order a Coke Zero everywhere I go. That is just what I drink, but it has turned out to be useful. Same product, same price band, roughly identical everywhere, which makes it a control rather than a dish. It never counts towards a rating.

At one restaurant so far, it has been the best thing on the table.
