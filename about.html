AboutNotes from the Foothills

Based near the Mata Chintpurni Mandir, separated by almost two hundred kilometres from everything I like. My entire horizon is swallowed by the Dhauladhar mountains, and I spend my time pointing a simple phone camera at them.

What I do all day

I work in operations. In practice that means I spend my day looking at what went wrong yesterday, working out whether it will go wrong again tomorrow, and building something small so that it does not.

Most of that something small is spreadsheets and scripts. A dashboard nobody asked for that turns out to be the thing everyone opens first. A report that used to take an afternoon and now takes a keystroke. It is unglamorous work and I am better at it than at anything else I have tried.

It is also the reason this site looks like this. I wrote every page of it by hand. There is no newsletter box, no cookie banner, no tracker counting how far down you scrolled. If something here is broken, I broke it.

What is on here

Three things, and they are not related except by being mine.

Photographs. Mostly one mountain range, at an unreasonable number of different hours. All of them taken within a few kilometres of my house. I have not edited them in any way worth mentioning and some of the best ones I did nothing to deserve.

Food reviews. I am in Tricity often, seeing friends I have had for years, and mostly we eat and drink. I score every restaurant properly and publish all the working, so you can disagree with the number rather than just with me. How I rate explains the whole thing, including why I am in a position to say a place was bad.

Weekly notes. Not started. When they exist they will be short pieces about whatever I have been building or getting wrong.

Things worth knowing

I like almost everything. My friends once sent back a pasta at Pizza Hut that I had no problem with whatsoever, and that happens more often than it should. It is a genuinely useful trait for someone who scores restaurants, because when I finally say a meal was not worth the money, it took some doing.

I did not start paying attention to any of this until I began writing reviews on Google Maps and had to explain myself. Turns out having to justify an opinion in public is the fastest way to find out whether you had one.

I started this site because one meal was good enough that I wanted to say so somewhere that would last. Appreciating something properly is harder than complaining about it, and that is still the reason this exists.
