How I rateOne question per dish, and the odds you order well

The Reorder

Every dish I eat gets one question. Would I order that again? Yes or no. Not whether it was clever, not whether it photographed well, not whether the chef meant something by it. Would I spend another meal on it.

The restaurant's rating is the answer across the whole table. Four dishes, two I'd have again, that is 2 in 4.

That number is the odds you order well if you walk in and pick something. It is the thing I would actually want to know before sitting down, and no rating system in food has ever told me.

Why odds and not a percentage

Saying seventy-five per cent when I ate four dishes pretends to a precision I do not have. 3 in 4 is simply what happened. When I go back and eat five more, it becomes 6 in 9, and you can see exactly how much eating the number is built on.

Why this works everywhere

Money is not universal. You cannot compare a ₹40 plate of momos to a ₹3,000 tasting menu on price, and rating both out of ten produces a number that means two different things.

Dishes are universal. Two plates at a roadside dhaba, twelve courses in a dining room, one steel thali at a wedding. Every one of them puts food in front of you, and every one of them can be asked the same question with the same meaning.

Would I go back?

Yes or no, printed on every review. No hedging, no "maybe if I was passing". It is the only judgement I make that cannot be argued down by a number, and it is the one most people actually want.

The thirty

Underneath the Reorder sits a score out of thirty. Food out of ten, value out of ten, service out of five, room out of five. Whole numbers, no halves.

Food · 9–10 best in its category · 7–8 clearly above the norm · 5–6 competent and forgettable · 3–4 noticeably off · 1–2 bad

Value · 9–10 would have paid more · 7–8 the bill never crossed my mind · 5–6 priced about right · 3–4 I felt the bill · 1–2 robbed

Service · 5 attentive without hovering · 4 smooth · 3 functional · 2 slow or absent · 1 bad

Room · 5 would sit two hours · 4 comfortable · 3 fine · 2 too loud, bright or cramped · 1 unpleasant

The Reorder is the food. The thirty is the whole evening. They are supposed to disagree, and when they do, the gap between them is the review. A kitchen can produce two dishes I would happily eat again on a night that was still the worst of the year, because of a waiter who would not look at me and four separate requests for water. One number could never say that. Two can.

Two rules

A dish I have no view on does not count. If I did not eat enough of something to judge it, it is skipped entirely, in or out of the total. Padding the denominator would be as dishonest as padding the score.

Coke Zero does not count. I order one everywhere I go, which makes it a control rather than a dish. It stays out of the maths and in the writing.
